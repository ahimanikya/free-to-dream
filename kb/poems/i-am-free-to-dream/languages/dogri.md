## Why this musical direction

The solitary opening can gradually become a shared song. Keep the response short enough that the original intimate poem never becomes a crowd chorus.

## Cultural grounding

Research on Dogri bhaakhs discusses group folk song and themes of separation. This new arrangement borrows the idea of voices answering across space, not a complete traditional bhaakh form. [Songs of Separation — A Study of Select Dogri Folk Songs, Bhaakhs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348960430_SONGS_OF_SEPARATION_A_STUDY_OF_SELECT_DOGRI_FOLK_SONGS_BHAAKHS)

The arrangement below is a creative proposal for this poem. It is not a claim to represent every tradition in the language, or to reproduce a traditional composition. Regional instruments, phrasing and song forms overlap across communities.

## Voice, rhythm and arrangement

Lead voice, a distant unison answer on existing words, soft flute and a quiet modern drone. Light dholak may enter only in the bridge; the vocal space is the defining feature.

## Emotional shape

Open with a solitary call. Leave the potter line entirely solo. Introduce the answering voice at the final dream-weaving refrain, making togetherness the destination.
