# Meaning reference for the multilingual adaptations

Original poem by Ahimanikya Satapathy. This document is a translation aid, not lyrics to paste into Suno.

## Original Odia wording supplied by the author

ମୋତେ ସପ୍ନ ଦେଖିବାକୁ ମନା ନାହିଁ
ମନା ନାହିଁ ମଝି ରାସ୍ତାରେ ହଜି ଜିବାକୁ
ଧୃବତାରା ଖୋଜିଦେବ ରାସ୍ତା ।

ସାଉଁଟି ସାଉଁଟି ଜାବୁଡି ଧରିଛି ସଂସାରକୁ
ଜୋଡି ଦେବାର ଆଶାରେ,
ସଜେଇ ଦେବାର ଆଶାରେ ।

କୁଂଭକାର ମୁଁ, ଗଠିଦେବା ସିଖିଛି
ରଂଗେଈ ଦେଈ,
ତୁମ ମୁଖରେ ହସ ବୁଣିବା ସିଖିଛି ।

ଆସ ପାହାଡ ଚଢିବା
ଆସ ଜନ୍ହ ଦେଖିବା
ବାଦଲ ସାଂଗେ କାଂଧ ମିଳେଈ
ଝଡିବାର ମଂତ୍ର ସିଖିବା ।

ମୋତେ ସପ୍ନ ଦେଖିବାକୁ ମନା ନାହିଁ ।
ମନା ନାହିଁ ଗୁଂଥିଦେବାକୁ
ତୁମ ସପ୍ନକୁ ମୋ ସପ୍ନରେ ।

## Intended meaning and essential images

- The speaker is free to dream and free to become lost along the way. The North Star will show the way onward.
- Gathering the world, piece by piece, the speaker holds it close, hoping to join or mend it and make it beautiful. This is not a gathering of people or a social meeting.
- The speaker is a potter, a maker who has learned to give form and colour and to sow a smile on the beloved's face. A smile blooming is an acceptable lyrical adaptation of the sowing image.
- The speaker invites the beloved to climb mountains and look at the moon. Together, shoulder to shoulder with the clouds, they learn the mantra of falling or shedding as rain. The rain image interprets the original in its cloud context; retain its feeling of release and generosity.
- The final freedom is to weave the beloved's dreams into the speaker's dreams.

## Musical structure and pauses

Intro: the opening stanza.

Verse 1: gathering and embracing the world. Keep a distinct breath between the repeated gathering phrases.

Verse 2: the potter. Keep a full blank line after “I am a potter.” The instruments should sustain beneath this pause.

Chorus: repeat the opening stanza.

Bridge: mountains, moon, clouds, rain.

Final Chorus: the last stanza about dreaming and weaving dreams together.

Soft Outro: repeat the final weaving image.
