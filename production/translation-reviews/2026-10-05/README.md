# Translation review — 5 October 2026

Scope: the **I Am Free to Dream collection in Poem Without Borders**. All 102 language pages were screened; all 29 existing target-language base lyrics were read against the five original Odia stanzas. The Odia original is preserved. The other 72 pages are adaptation briefs, not translations.

## What changed

- **French:** `en chemin` → `à mi-chemin`, restoring midway in both refrains.
- **Marathi:** `वाटेत` → `अर्ध्या वाटेत`, restoring halfway in both refrains.
- **Kannada:** `ಒಂದೊಂದಾಗಿ... ಒಂದೊಂದಾಗಿ...` → `ಕೂಡಿಸುತ್ತಾ... ಕೂಡಿಸುತ್ತಾ...`, restoring an explicit gathering verb and updating the exact quoted phrase in the style instruction.
- The shared meaning guide and 72 English adaptation scaffolds now explicitly retain **midway along the road**. Their historical machine-translation provenance is untouched.

These are limited fidelity edits to unrecorded drafts, not new arrangements or native-speaker approvals. Existing title, stanza/voice labels, potter pauses, musical rationale and production settings remain intact. No published or catalogued recording lyric was changed.

## What should stay

The meaning guide expressly allows **a smile blooming** for the original smile-sowing image. That is not an error to fix mechanically. The chosen English “Piece by piece” responds to the author's dislike of “Gathering, gathering.” Tamil's potter pause and longer smile-sowing phrase stay. Sambalpuri and Bhojpuri keep their regional voices and selected vocal arrangement. Masculine forms in the male-led versions do not establish the source speaker's gender; any new female-led arrangement needs its own agreement pass.

## Open editorial questions

- **Filipino:** “Makisabay sa mga ulap” accompanies the clouds but loses explicit shoulder-to-shoulder imagery. Obtain a natural Filipino alternative before changing a recorded line.
- **Konkani, Sanskrit, Swahili:** the road remains but the midway detail is not explicit. A fluent/specialist reader should supply the locally natural repair. No guessed replacement has been inserted.
- **English:** midway and mantra are condensed; “Thread by thread” is added. Retain the selected song. A future literary edition can make separate choices.
- **Mandarin:** the bridge drops mantra/secret and adds gentleness. Treat this as a documented song adaptation, not a fabricated grammatical fault.
- **Dogri, Haryanvi, Maithili, Konkani, Sindhi, Sambalpuri and Sanskrit:** prioritize variety-aware or specialist review. Their complete meaning comparison is recorded, but their idiom and grammar have not been independently certified.

Every language has specific observations and a follow-up in `review.json` and the readable `review.html`. A retained draft means no confident correction was justified in this reading; it does not mean native-approved.

## Evidence and versioning

`before/` preserves exact pre-review source and language files. `revised/` contains only revised files, `changes.patch` shows every edit, and `manifest.json` stores before/after SHA-256 file hashes. Hashes identify the exact review scope and guard against overwriting subsequent work. `application.json`, when present, records local application; it is not publication evidence.

This is a same-assistant review, with **zero independent fluent-speaker reviews** and no new author approval. All 29 target pages retain `needs-native-review`. No generated sound, recorded performance, or historical variation was certified. The repository's production packets preserve historical lyrics; do not rewrite them retroactively. Regenerate future packets from revised pages after resolving the relevant review questions.
